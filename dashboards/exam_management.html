Architectural Decisions (with Tradeoffs)
Major decisions from Granola synthesis + Nipun alignment
Decision 1
Folder structure: Flat + Smart Views

Chose flat folder model (no unlimited nesting) + Smart Views for filtering over hierarchical structure.
Tradeoff: Simplicity + accessibility for all users vs. power-user folder control. Research-focused DCEs may want hierarchies; simplified model serves broader audience.
Source: Granola (Oct consultant input)
Decision 2
Question versioning: Draft→Saved→Active + Archive

Three distinct states for question lifecycle, aligning with document paradigm (Google Docs).
Tradeoff: "Saved" requires user education vs. ExamSoft's "Published." Small semantic difference, large UX clarity gain.
Source: Granola + internal team alignment
Decision 3
Archive, don't delete

Questions never deleted; archived only. Preserves audit trail + enables accreditation reporting.
Tradeoff: Adds complexity in question bank (archive filter needed) vs. faculty expectation of delete button.
Source: Accreditation requirement (locked questions for audit)
Decision 4
Phase 1: 7 question types only

MCQ, Multiple Answer, Matching, Short Answer, True/False, Essay, Calculation. No image-based or complex question types in MVP.
Tradeoff: Covers 95% of healthcare education use cases vs. ExamSoft 15+ types. Acceptable scope cut.
Source: Nipun + Vishal scope negotiation
Decision 5
Offline exam download: Cut from MVP, March add

Push offline capability to March limited availability (LA) sprint, not January MVP.
Tradeoff: Risks faculty comparison to ExamSoft ("Why can't I download?") vs. January go-live confidence.
Source: Arti (leadership decision); team consensus on timeline
Decision 6
Accessibility: In-app zoom + WCAG 2.1 AA (within lockdown)

Cannot use OS-level zoom in lockdown browser; building zoom toolbar within exam student UX.
Tradeoff: Added complexity vs. accreditation requirement (ADA Title III) + competitive differentiation.
Source: Accessibility audit + OCR precedent

Platform Signal
Faculty grading workflow is consistent across disciplines. Post-exam: (1) Faculty reviews class performance + item analysis, (2) Meets with chair on poor questions, (3) Adjusts scores, (4) Pushes to LMS, (5) Schedules exam review with students.
Source: Jul 23 Exam Management weekly call (consultant input synthesis)
Design Opportunity
Score adjustment workflows must support class-level override + individual student override. Faculty flagging a bad question should auto-update all student scores; faculty can also override individual student scores separately. Design must make this clear.
Source: Jul 23 Exam Management weekly call (faculty workflow mapping)

Risks & Mitigation
Known blockers and contingency plans
CRITICAL: Offline exam download gap (Jan MVP)
Faculty will compare to ExamSoft ("Can't download exams, so I'm stuck with you"). March timeline confirmed; team aware. Mitigation: Alpha customers briefed on roadmap; account managers prepared with messaging ("Unique placement context in Exxat makes offline less critical; we're adding it in LA").
HIGH: Accessibility baseline gap
Lockdown browser blocks OS zoom + screen readers. Exxat building in-app solutions; not perfect parity with standard browser. Mitigation: Formal accessibility audit (Oct 2026); WCAG AA certification target; legal team briefed on ADA risk.
HIGH: Faculty adoption friction
Question Bank UX must be easier than ExamSoft or faculty will ignore it. Zero formal usability testing yet. Mitigation: Usability test 5 faculty (Nov 2026, 8 weeks pre-MVP); iterate if needed.
MEDIUM: Question bank lock-in risk
Questions authored in Exxat don't port to ExamSoft. If customer switches, all question work is lost. Mitigation: Document exit path (bulk export option); positioning focuses on Exxat ecosystem benefits (placement context), not trap.
MEDIUM: Competitive response (ExamSoft AI)
ExamSoft may add AI question generation before Exxat. Timeline unclear. Mitigation: Exxat AI embedded naturally (not labeled); differentiation is Exxat's placement context + accreditation narrative, not AI alone.
